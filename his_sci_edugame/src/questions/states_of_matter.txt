How many states of matter are there?
a. Three
b. Two
c. Four
d. Five
Explanation: The three main states of matter are solid, liquid, and gas.

What happens to water when it freezes?
a. It becomes a solid
b. It becomes a gas
c. It disappears
d. It becomes a liquid
Explanation: When water freezes, it changes from a liquid to a solid (ice).

What is the process called when a solid turns directly into a gas?
a. Sublimation
b. Melting
c. Evaporation
d. Condensation
Explanation: Sublimation is when a solid turns directly into a gas without becoming a liquid first, like dry ice.

Particles in a solid are:
a. Close together and vibrating
b. Far apart and moving fast
c. Moving freely
d. Not moving at all
Explanation: In solids, particles are tightly packed together and can only vibrate in place.

What happens to the volume of a gas when you heat it?
a. It increases
b. It decreases
c. It stays the same
d. It disappears
Explanation: When you heat a gas, the particles move faster and spread out, causing the volume to increase.

What is the process called when a liquid turns into a gas?
a. Evaporation
b. Condensation
c. Freezing
d. Melting
Image: evaporation_process.png
Explanation: Evaporation is when a liquid changes into a gas, like when water turns into water vapor.

What happens to particles when a solid melts?
a. They gain energy and move more freely
b. They lose energy and slow down
c. They stay in the same position
d. They disappear
Explanation: When a solid melts, particles gain heat energy and begin to move more freely, changing from a solid to a liquid state.

What is the freezing point of water at standard atmospheric pressure?
a. 0 degrees Celsius
b. 32 degrees Celsius
c. 100 degrees Celsius
d. -10 degrees Celsius
Image: freezing_point.png
Explanation: Water freezes at 0°C (32°F) at standard atmospheric pressure, changing from liquid to solid ice.

What state of matter has a definite volume but no definite shape?
a. Liquid
b. Solid
c. Gas
d. Plasma
Explanation: Liquids have a definite volume (they take up a specific amount of space) but no definite shape (they take the shape of their container).

What happens to the temperature of a substance during a phase change?
a. It stays constant
b. It increases
c. It decreases
d. It changes randomly
Explanation: During a phase change (like melting or boiling), the temperature stays constant because energy is being used to change the state, not to increase temperature.

What is plasma?
a. A state of matter with charged particles
b. A type of liquid
c. A type of solid
d. A type of gas
Image: plasma.png
Explanation: Plasma is the fourth state of matter, consisting of charged particles. It's found in stars and lightning.

What happens when you compress a gas?
a. The particles get closer together
b. The particles move faster
c. The particles disappear
d. Nothing happens
Explanation: When you compress a gas, you force the particles closer together, reducing the volume while the number of particles stays the same.

What is the boiling point of water at sea level?
a. 100 degrees Celsius
b. 0 degrees Celsius
c. 50 degrees Celsius
d. 200 degrees Celsius
Explanation: Water boils at 100°C (212°F) at sea level, changing from liquid to gas throughout the entire volume.

What state of matter has particles that are far apart and move very fast?
a. Gas
b. Solid
c. Liquid
d. All of the above
Explanation: In gases, particles are far apart and move very quickly in all directions, which is why gases expand to fill their containers.

What happens to ice when it melts?
a. It changes from solid to liquid
b. It changes from liquid to gas
c. It changes from gas to liquid
d. It stays the same
Explanation: Melting is the process where a solid (ice) gains enough heat energy to change into a liquid (water).

What is the process called when a gas turns directly into a solid?
a. Deposition
b. Sublimation
c. Evaporation
d. Condensation
Explanation: Deposition is when a gas changes directly into a solid, like when water vapor forms frost on a cold surface.

What property allows liquids to flow?
a. Particles can slide past each other
b. Particles are fixed in place
c. Particles are very far apart
d. Particles don't move
Explanation: In liquids, particles are close together but can slide past each other, allowing liquids to flow and take the shape of their container.

What happens to the density of water when it freezes?
a. It decreases (ice is less dense)
b. It increases
c. It stays the same
d. It disappears
Explanation: Water is unusual because ice is less dense than liquid water, which is why ice floats on water.
