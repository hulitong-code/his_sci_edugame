What is the process called when water turns into water vapor?
a. Evaporation
b. Condensation
c. Precipitation
d. Collection

Explanation: Evaporation is when liquid water turns into water vapor (gas).

What happens during condensation in the water cycle?
a. Water vapor turns into liquid
b. Liquid turns into ice
c. Ice turns into water
d. Water flows downhill
Explanation: Condensation is when water vapor cools down and turns back into liquid water, forming clouds.

Which stage of the water cycle involves water falling from the sky?
a. Precipitation
b. Evaporation
c. Condensation
d. Transpiration
Explanation: Precipitation is when water falls from clouds as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

What is transpiration in the water cycle?
a. Water evaporating from plants
b. Water freezing in clouds
c. Water flowing in rivers
d. Water collecting in oceans
Explanation: Transpiration is when plants release water vapor through their leaves into the atmosphere.

Where does most of the Earth's water come from in the water cycle?
a. Oceans
b. Rivers
c. Lakes
d. Underground
Explanation: Oceans cover about 71% of Earth's surface and are the primary source of water in the water cycle.

What is the main source of energy that drives the water cycle?
a. The Sun
b. The Moon
c. Wind
d. Gravity
Explanation: The Sun provides the energy that heats water and causes evaporation, which drives the entire water cycle.

What happens to water molecules during evaporation?
a. They gain energy and move faster
b. They lose energy and slow down
c. They combine with other molecules
d. They break apart into atoms
Explanation: During evaporation, water molecules absorb heat energy from the Sun, causing them to move faster and change from liquid to gas.

What forms when water vapor cools and condenses in the atmosphere?
a. Clouds
b. Rain
c. Snow
d. Ice
Image: clouds_formation.png
Explanation: When water vapor cools, it condenses into tiny water droplets that form clouds in the atmosphere.

What is the term for water that flows over the land surface into rivers and lakes?
a. Runoff
b. Infiltration
c. Percolation
d. Collection
Image: runoff.png
Explanation: Runoff is water that flows over the ground surface when the soil is saturated or during heavy rain.

What percentage of Earth's water is fresh water?
a. About 3%

c. About 25%
d. About 50%
Explanation: Only about 3% of Earth's water is fresh water, and most of that is frozen in ice caps and glaciers.

What is groundwater?
a. Water stored underground in soil and rock
b. Water in the ocean
c. Water in rivers
d. Water in clouds
Image: groundwater.png
Explanation: Groundwater is water that has infiltrated the ground and is stored in spaces between soil particles and in rock formations.

What happens during the collection stage of the water cycle?
a. Water accumulates in oceans, lakes, and rivers
b. Water evaporates into the air
c. Water falls as precipitation
d. Water is absorbed by plants
Explanation: Collection is when water gathers in bodies of water like oceans, lakes, and rivers, completing the water cycle.

What is the process called when water seeps into the ground?
a. Infiltration
b. Evaporation
c. Condensation
d. Transpiration
Explanation: Infiltration is when water on the ground surface seeps down into the soil and rock layers.

Why is the water cycle important for life on Earth?
a. It distributes water and nutrients around the planet
b. It creates weather patterns
c. It cleans the water
d. All of the above
Explanation: The water cycle is essential because it distributes water globally, creates weather, and helps clean and recycle water.

What happens to water when it reaches 100 degrees Celsius at sea level?
a. It boils and turns into water vapor
b. It freezes into ice
c. It stays as liquid
d. It disappears
Explanation: At 100°C (212°F), water reaches its boiling point and rapidly changes from liquid to water vapor (steam).

What is the main difference between evaporation and transpiration?
a. Evaporation is from surfaces, transpiration is from plants
b. Evaporation is faster than transpiration
c. Transpiration needs heat, evaporation doesn't
d. They are the same process
Explanation: Evaporation is when water changes from liquid to vapor from any surface, while transpiration is specifically when plants release water vapor through their leaves.
