What process do plants use to make their own food?
a. Photosynthesis
b. Respiration
c. Digestion
d. Transpiration
Image: photosynthesis.png
Explanation: Photosynthesis is the process where plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make glucose (sugar) and oxygen.

What gas do plants absorb from the air during photosynthesis?
a. Carbon dioxide
b. Oxygen
c. Nitrogen
d. Hydrogen
Explanation: Plants absorb carbon dioxide from the air and use it along with water and sunlight to make food.

What do plants release during photosynthesis?
a. Oxygen
b. Carbon dioxide
c. Nitrogen
d. Water vapor
Explanation: Plants release oxygen as a byproduct of photosynthesis, which is essential for animals to breathe.

What part of the plant absorbs water and nutrients from the soil?
a. Roots
b. Leaves
c. Stem
d. Flowers
Explanation: Roots anchor the plant and absorb water and nutrients from the soil.

What is the green pigment in plants that captures sunlight called?
a. Chlorophyll
b. Chloroplast
c. Cellulose
d. Stomata
Image: chlorophyll.png
Explanation: Chlorophyll is the green pigment in plant cells that captures light energy for photosynthesis.

What are the tiny openings on plant leaves called?
a. Stomata
b. Chloroplasts
c. Roots
d. Veins
Image: stomata.png
Explanation: Stomata are small openings on leaves that allow plants to take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen and water vapor.

What do plants need for photosynthesis besides sunlight?
a. Water and carbon dioxide
b. Only water
c. Only carbon dioxide
d. Soil and air
Explanation: For photosynthesis, plants need sunlight, water (from roots), and carbon dioxide (from air through stomata).

What is the main product that plants make during photosynthesis?
a. Glucose (sugar)
b. Oxygen only
c. Water
d. Carbon dioxide
Explanation: The main product of photosynthesis is glucose (a type of sugar), which plants use for energy. Oxygen is a byproduct.

What part of the plant carries water and nutrients from roots to leaves?
a. Stem
b. Leaves
c. Flowers
d. Roots
Explanation: The stem contains tubes (xylem and phloem) that transport water and nutrients throughout the plant.

What is the process called when plants lose water through their leaves?
a. Transpiration
b. Photosynthesis
c. Respiration
d. Germination
Explanation: Transpiration is when water evaporates from plant leaves, helping to pull water up from the roots.

What do plants use glucose for?
a. Energy and growth
b. Only for making oxygen
c. Only for storage
d. Nothing
Explanation: Plants use glucose (sugar) for energy to grow, reproduce, and carry out all their life processes.

What is germination?
a. When a seed starts to grow
b. When a plant flowers
c. When a plant dies
d. When a plant makes seeds
Explanation: Germination is the process when a seed begins to sprout and grow into a new plant.

What are the three main parts of a plant?
a. Roots, stem, and leaves
b. Only roots and leaves
c. Only stem and flowers
d. Seeds, flowers, and fruits
Explanation: The three main parts of a plant are roots (anchor and absorb), stem (support and transport), and leaves (photosynthesis).

What is the purpose of flowers in plants?
a. Reproduction (making seeds)
b. Only for decoration
c. Only for attracting insects
d. To make food
Explanation: Flowers are the reproductive part of plants, containing the structures needed to produce seeds and create new plants.

What happens to plants at night when there's no sunlight?
a. They respire (use oxygen and release carbon dioxide)
b. They stop all activities
c. They only photosynthesize
d. They grow faster
Explanation: At night, plants can't photosynthesize, so they respire (breathe) like animals, using oxygen and releasing carbon dioxide.

What is the waxy layer on plant leaves called?
a. Cuticle
b. Chlorophyll
c. Stomata
d. Epidermis
Explanation: The cuticle is a waxy layer on leaves that helps prevent water loss and protects the plant.

What do plant roots do besides absorbing water?
a. Anchor the plant and store food
b. Only absorb water
c. Only make food
d. Only produce oxygen
Explanation: Roots anchor plants in the ground, absorb water and nutrients, and can store food for the plant.

What is pollination?
a. The transfer of pollen from one flower to another
b. When a seed grows
c. When a plant makes food
d. When a plant loses leaves
Explanation: Pollination is when pollen (containing male reproductive cells) is transferred to the female part of a flower, enabling seed production.
